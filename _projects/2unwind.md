Unwind is a set of voice-controlled exercises designed to help working professionals better understand others and navigate conflict in collaborative work environments.

The project explores how conversational interfaces can support emotional reflection, perspective-taking, and difficult workplace conversations.

### Why I Built It

In modern work environments, knowledge workers are expected to collaborate across different personalities, goals, communication styles, and creative opinions. But many people are never directly taught how to navigate conflict, repair misunderstanding, or prepare for difficult conversations.

My motivation for this project came from moments in my own professional life when I wished I had been better prepared. During those times, I looked for books, workshops, and other resources that reflected similar situations. They helped me understand, improve, and heal, but they were not always available in the moments when I needed support.

This led to the core question behind Unwind:

**What if a voice interface could guide someone through a moment of conflict reflection before a difficult conversation?**

The goal was not to replace human support. It was to prototype an accessible tool that could help people slow down, understand another person’s perspective, and prepare for a more thoughtful conversation.

### My Role

I designed and prototyped the voice interaction experience. My work included:

- Researched conflict resolution approaches through conversation with a professional mediator
- Designed the conversation flow for guided reflection exercises
- Built the prototype with Dialogflow, Actions on Google, and Google Assistant
- Tested the experience with users and iterated on the flow
- Explored how voice interaction could support emotional reflection without feeling clinical or judgmental

### Research & Design Process

To better understand how people work through conflict, I spoke at length with professional mediator Colleen M. Maeder. Conversations with her helped me understand that conflict resolution often starts before the actual conversation: with naming the situation, understanding each person’s needs, and separating assumptions from observable facts.

Based on that process, I designed a voice-guided flow that helped users reflect on:

- what happened
- what they felt
- what the other person might need or believe
- what they wanted from the conversation
- how they could approach the situation more constructively

### Design Challenge

The main challenge was designing a voice experience for a sensitive emotional context.

Conflict is personal. A tool like this needed to feel supportive without pretending to be a therapist, mediator, or authority figure. The tone had to be calm, simple, and nonjudgmental.

The design challenge was:

**How can a voice interface help someone slow down and reflect without making the experience feel awkward, scripted, or overly formal?**

### Reflection

Unwind helped me see conversation design as a people-centered design problem. The hardest part was not only making the voice flow work technically, but shaping the interaction so people felt safe enough to reflect honestly.

This project deepened my interest in designing technology that supports understanding, emotional clarity, and more thoughtful human interaction.
